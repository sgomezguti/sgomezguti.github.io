About

I am a Computational Life Scientist with a Ph.D. in Plant Pathology (Computational Life Science) from Purdue University and a B.Sc. in Agronomic Engineering. I specialize in developing systems-level molecular network models and integrating multi-omics datasets—including genomics, transcriptomics, proteomics, and phenomics—to establish mechanistic, data-driven links between genotypes and complex phenotypes.

My research focuses on applying state-of-the-art AI-driven structural biology tools (such as AlphaFold3 and FoldSeek) alongside high-resolution population genomics to discover core molecular regulators, predict adaptive evolutionary trajectories, and model functional cellular trade-offs. I enjoy working at the interface of biology, data science, and agronomy, translating complex large-scale omics modeling into predictive strategies and sustainable agricultural solutions.

Research

My research program is built around two key intersecting pillars:

1. Pathogen Evolution & Population Genomics

Utilizing laboratory experimental evolution and population-level comparative genomics to decode how plant-associated microbes adapt to hosts, climate conditions, and environmental pressures [source: 1, 23]. I use these evolutionary datasets to develop predictive models capable of forecasting and preventing disease outbreaks before they happen.

2. AI-Driven Molecular Target Identification

Applying deep learning structural models (AlphaFold2/3) and machine-learning prioritization scoring to map complex host-pathogen molecular interaction networks [source: 1, 10, 25]. This allows us to discover key genetic regulators and target susceptibility proteins to design precise, ecology-based biocontrol and breeding strategies.
